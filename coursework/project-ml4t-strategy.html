Strategy Evaluation

Coursework / ML4T / Strategy Evaluation

What I Did

This was the capstone project. I built two complete trading strategies for JPM stock and compared their performance against a buy-and-hold benchmark. The Manual Strategy used human-coded rules combining Price/SMA, RSI, and MACD indicators to make long/short decisions. The Strategy Learner used a BagLearner ensemble of Random Trees trained on the same three indicators, learning to classify future price movements into BUY/HOLD/SELL signals. I evaluated both strategies in-sample (2008-2009) and out-of-sample (2010-2011) to test for overfitting.

What I Learned

The in-sample results make the Strategy Learner look superhuman. A 164.4% cumulative return on JPM during the 2008-2009 period, when the benchmark only achieved 1.23%, is staggering. But the real test is out-of-sample. On 2010-2011 data the Strategy Learner essentially broke even (-0.02%), while the benchmark lost 8.36%. This is the key insight: it is not about in-sample performance. It is about out-of-sample robustness. The Strategy Learner avoided the benchmark's losses entirely.

The Manual Strategy was designed around three specific rules: enter long when Price/SMA crosses above 0.95 with RSI confirmation between 30-50, enter short when it drops below 1.05 with RSI confirmation between 50-70, and use MACD histogram to filter momentum direction. These rules worked during the volatile 2008-2009 period, but the manual approach struggled when market dynamics shifted in 2010-2011. The Strategy Learner, by contrast, discovered non-obvious threshold combinations that generalized better.

The most surprising discovery was that the Strategy Learner achieved lower volatility than both the benchmark and the manual strategy. A standard deviation of 0.0093 in-sample and 0.0082 out-of-sample means the AI wasn't just finding profitable trades - it was systematically avoiding the worst drawdowns. This is where machine learning truly shines: it captures second-order effects that human intuition misses.

Commission and impact costs were devastating to high-frequency manual strategies. My initial manual implementation made too many trades, and the $9.95 commission plus 0.5% impact per trade eroded all alpha. I had to redesign the rules with a "dead zone" between 0.95 and 1.05 to limit trades to genuine signals. This single change improved the manual strategy's Sharpe ratio by 40%.

This project tied together everything from the course: indicators (P6), simulation with transaction costs (P5), supervised learners (P3), bagging ensembles (P3), and out-of-sample testing guidelines. The end-to-end system I built, from data ingestion to trade execution to performance reporting, is the most complete piece of software I've written so far in the program. It gave me real confidence that I can build production-quality trading infrastructure, not just toy models.
