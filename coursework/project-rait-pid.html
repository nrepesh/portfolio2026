Drone Control (PID)

Coursework / RAIT / Drone Control (PID)

Problem

Fly a simulated dual-rotor drone from rest to a target elevation and horizontal position, then hold a hover. Thrust comes from rotor RPM; horizontal motion comes from roll, induced by differential thrust between the two rotors.

Simulator telemetry: target versus actual elevation and horizontal position, thrust and roll controller output, and per-rotor RPM.

Approach

  • Separate PID controllers for thrust and roll, each returning a change in actuator value rather than an absolute setpoint
  • Derivative term damps the overshoot that proportional control alone guarantees
  • Integral term cancels the steady-state offset left by a persistent disturbance such as rotor droop or added payload
  • Integral accumulation suspended while the actuator is saturated, preventing wind-up and the overshoot on unwind
  • Gains found by twiddle, a coordinate-ascent search, rather than tuned by hand

Thrust and roll are coupled, since rolling to translate tilts thrust off vertical, so tuning the two axes independently does not converge.
